Executive summary

  • Nairobi has taken concrete steps on environmental and cardiovascular health, adopting an Air Quality Action Plan in 2020 and supporting the Healthy Heart Africa Project in Kibera, which has screened over 100,000 residents and started treatment for more than 2,500 hypertension patients while building community awareness and health‑worker capacity [10][5][5][5].
  • The city’s over‑4‑million population creates high demand for services, and stark socioeconomic disparities exist in perceived air quality, with only 0.3% of low‑SES respondents rating it as good versus 12.2% of high‑SES respondents [11][10].
  • Nationally, cardiovascular disease accounts for a substantial share of the health burden: 25% of hospital admissions and 13% of deaths, 24% of adults live with hypertension, and over 90% of diagnosed hypertensives lack disease control; premature CVD mortality was 8% in 2019 and overall CVD mortality 13.8% [2][1].
  • Kenya’s health system benefits from strong stakeholder engagement, with WHO providing technical support to county health systems and CDC collaborating on surveillance, laboratory capacity, and emergency operations across the country, though workforce shortages remain severe (only 1 doctor and 10 nurses per 10,000 people) [9][8][14].
  • Key evidence gaps limit city‑level planning: there is no Nairobi‑specific data on CVD burden, health‑system capacity, or policy implementation, and the most recent programme and policy information dates back to 2014‑2015, requiring updated local assessments to guide interventions [2][14][5][7]
